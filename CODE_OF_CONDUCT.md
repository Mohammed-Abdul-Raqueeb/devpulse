# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is acting in good faith, and give them a reason to assume the
same of you.

## What is expected

- Keep criticism on the code, not the person. "This query will table-scan at a million rows" is
  useful; "this is amateur work" is not.
- Accept that people arrive with different levels of experience. A question that seems obvious to
  you took someone else effort to ask in public.
- Be explicit about disagreement and move on once it is settled. A maintainer's decision on scope
  is final, even when you are right about the tradeoff.
- Credit people's work, including in commit messages and release notes.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, in public or private.
- Personal attacks, insults, or sustained hostility.
- Demeaning comments about who someone is — nationality, ethnicity, sex, gender identity or
  expression, sexual orientation, disability, religion, age, appearance, or anything else of the
  kind.
- Publishing someone's private information without their permission.
- Deliberately derailing discussion, or repeatedly reopening a settled decision to wear people down.

## Scope

This applies in the issue tracker, pull requests, commit messages, code comments and anywhere else
the project is being discussed, including when representing the project elsewhere.

## Reporting

Report a problem through GitHub's private vulnerability and abuse reporting on this repository, or
by opening an issue if it can be discussed publicly.

Reports are read by the maintainer. Expect an acknowledgement within a few days. Details stay
private, including from the person reported, unless disclosure is necessary to act on it.

## Consequences

Depending on severity and whether it is repeated: a private warning, a request to edit or withdraw
a comment, a temporary block, or a permanent ban from the repository. Maintainers who do not uphold
this in good faith can expect the same treatment as anyone else.
